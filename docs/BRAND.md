Product name from Sat Nov 14: **Unspoken** (working name; say "working name" on the title slide). Replaces "CareSignal", which is an existing healthcare company (remote patient monitoring, acquired by Lightbeam Health Solutions). The repo name, Railway project, URLs, and invite codes stay as they are. Rename steps: `docs/RENAME.md`.

Name check: a web search on Nov 14 found no health or caregiving product called Unspoken. That is not a trademark search. Before using the name beyond the buildathon, check the USPTO database and domains.

## Tagline
**Unspoken. Every need, finally heard.**

Alternatives by audience:
- Families: "Unspoken. What they can't say, we help you see."

- Title slide, shortest: "Unspoken. Distress is communication."

## Mission
To make sure no one's needs go unmet just because they couldn't say them.

## Vision
A world where everyone who cares for a person shares one understanding of them, so distress is understood instead of managed.

## What it is (one sentence)
Unspoken is one shared record of each hard moment, built from the voices of everyone who cares for a person who can't always say what they need, so families and care teams can see what led up to it and what actually helped.

## Positioning
- The differentiator is the shared episode record across caregivers: what led up to it, what was tried step by step, and what helped. Voice is the way in, not the claim.
- Do not claim to be the first voice logging app. Guiding Growth already offers voice logging for autism parents.
- The dementia flow follows the DICE approach (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate), which was tested in the WeCareAdvisor trial. Say so.
- Community rankings ("what helped other families") are the long-term vision and run on synthetic data today.
- See `docs/research/market_and_differentiation.md`.

## The story (pitch version, about 60 seconds)
When someone can't tell you what's wrong, they tell you another way. A child bites his hand at 2 AM. A mother paces and calls out every afternoon. We call it "behavior." It's communication, and the message goes unspoken.

The people around them each hold a piece of the answer. The home aide saw the small lunch. Dad was there for the bad night. The teacher noticed the loud assembly. But those pieces live in different heads, different phones, and a notebook nobody can find. So the same hard moment repeats, and everyone guesses.

Unspoken brings those pieces together. Anyone who cares for the person says what happened, in their own words and their own language. We turn it into one clear record: what led up to it, what was tried, step by step, and what actually helped. Over time, the pattern speaks for the person who couldn't.

We don't diagnose. We don't replace doctors or therapists. We help the people who love someone finally hear them.

## The story (short version)
Some people can't always say when they're in pain, hungry, or overwhelmed. It comes out as distress, and the people around them each see only a piece. Unspoken joins those pieces into one record, so families can see what's really going on and what helps.

## Closing line
"The pattern speaks for the person who couldn't."

## Why the name works
- It names the problem, not the technology. The need is unspoken; the app is how it gets heard.
- It covers both communities without naming a condition.
- It also covers the caregiver, whose own exhaustion often goes unspoken (check-in, 988).

## Wording rules
- The need is unspoken, not the person. Many autistic people communicate richly without speech. Say "can't always say what they need". Never "can't communicate", "nonverbal" as a label for everyone, or "has no voice".
- Never "we speak for them". Say "we help the people around them understand".
- Keep "Supports caregivers. Does not diagnose." on every insight screen and in the pitch.
- Use a real caregiver's story only with their permission, by role only, with no names or identifying details.
- No em-dashes in any copy.

## Usage
- Wordmark: "Unspoken" in sentence case. Not "UnSpoken", not "UNSPOKEN" in body text.
- First mention in a document: "Unspoken (working name)". After that: "Unspoken".
- App header: "Unspoken". Tagline under it where space allows: "Every need, finally heard."

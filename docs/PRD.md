# CareSignal PRD (buildathon scope)

## 1. One-liner
For caregivers of people who cannot say what they need, CareSignal turns a spoken sentence into a structured log, finds the patterns behind distress, and recommends strategies ranked by what actually worked for others. It supports caregivers and never diagnoses.

Pitch line: "Distress is communication. We help caregivers decode it, and every family's experience makes the next family's answer better."

## 2. Primary users (one engine, two modes)
- **Dementia Care mode.** Maria, a certified nursing aide on the afternoon shift at a memory care unit. She cares for 10 residents, has gloves on half the day, and never sees what the morning shift noticed. Mrs. Helen Patel (synthetic) gets agitated most afternoons and it is charted as a "behavior problem".
- **Autism Family mode.** Priya, parent of Arjun (synthetic, age 7, autistic). Mornings before school often end in meltdowns. She keeps scattered notes on her phone and cannot see a pattern or explain it clearly to his therapist.

Lead the demo with ONE mode (team decides; default Dementia Care) and show the other with a mode switch to prove it is one engine.

## 3. Evidence
NO EVIDENCE YET from interviews. PM collects 3 to 5 quotes at the event from people with elder care or autism-parent experience into `docs/research/`. Background: care literature treats agitation in dementia as communication of unmet needs, frequently pain (Alzheimer's Association training materials).

## 4. Core flow
Caregiver speaks -> AI formats a structured record -> pattern finder updates -> caregiver asks "why" -> grounded answer plus scored Winning Pattern.

1. Caregiver taps the mic and says one natural sentence.
2. Claude extracts a structured record using controlled vocabulary; a confirm card shows fields with low-confidence fields highlighted.
3. Caregiver taps Save (or edits a field). Record stored with the raw transcript.
4. Person view shows timeline plus pattern cards with evidence counts.
5. Caregiver asks a question by voice or text; answer cites counts from that person's logs.
6. Answer includes ranked strategies with tier badges and family evidence.

## 5. Screens (5)
1. **Home / Log**: mode switch, person picker, big mic button, small tap-logger form fallback, confirm card.
2. **Person view**: header with person, timeline grouped by day and time band, 2 to 3 pattern cards (finding, evidence count, "possible things to check", link to matching strategies).
3. **Ask**: question box with mic, answer with evidence lines and linked entries, red-flag banner when triggered.
4. **Winning Patterns**: strategies for a selected pattern, ranked by score, tier badge, families, % improved, average drop, durability, disclaimer.
5. **About / guardrails** (small): what it does, what it never does, synthetic data note.

## 6. Why AI
Free speech from a busy caregiver is messy ("she was really worked up around 3, wouldn't eat"). AI turns it into consistent, countable fields in seconds, which makes cross-shift and cross-family pattern finding possible at all. AI also explains statistics in caregiver language and answers natural questions. The statistics themselves are plain code, so the system cannot hallucinate a pattern.

## 7. Data
All synthetic, generated by `scripts/seed.ts`:
- Dementia: 3 residents, 21 days of logs. Mrs. Patel has the planted pattern (afternoon agitation after skipped lunch). One other resident has a planted shift-change noise pattern.
- Autism: 2 children, 21 days. Arjun has the planted pattern (morning meltdowns after poor sleep plus routine change).
- 50 background families/residents with strategy trials and before/after outcomes, used for Winning Pattern scores. Scores precomputed by the seed script.
- Demo voice phrases cached (see Demo script).

## 8. Out of scope (do not build)
1. Login, accounts, roles, permissions (single demo user, mode switch only)
2. Live tried-it tracking and live score recomputation (scores come from seed data)
3. Shift handover summaries and therapist visit summaries (roadmap slide)
4. Sharing with co-parents, teachers, clinicians
5. Community posting, comments, free-text advice
6. Consent flows (described on slide only)
7. Spoken read-back and voice "save" command
8. Gap-filling follow-up questions
9. Integration with facility record systems
10. Native mobile app, push notifications, offline mode
11. Multilingual voice
12. Camera or sensor input
13. Fine-tuning any model

## 9. Demo script (90 seconds)
1. (10s) Mode = Dementia Care. Maria taps mic: "Mrs. Patel was agitated around 3, pacing and calling out. She skipped lunch. Crackers helped, settled after 20 minutes."
2. (10s) Confirm card shows: Helen Patel, agitation, moderate, 3:00 PM afternoon, pacing + calling out, context skipped_meal, response offered_snack, outcome calmed. Save.
3. (20s) Person view: pattern card lights up "Afternoon agitation followed a skipped lunch on 6 of 8 days in the last 3 weeks." Possible things to check: hunger, pain, fatigue.
4. (25s) Ask: "Why is she so agitated in the afternoons?" Answer cites counts and the cross-shift link ("morning shift logged a skipped lunch on 5 of those days").
5. (15s) Winning Patterns: "Scheduled mid-afternoon snack" Winning pattern, score 81, 27 residents, 74% improved, average drop 42%, held 6 weeks.
6. (10s) Flip mode to Autism Family, show Arjun's pattern card and one Winning Pattern ("visual morning schedule"). "One engine for anyone who cannot say what they need."

Cached demo phrases:
- D1 (above)
- D2: "Mr. Okafor was calling out during shift change, it was really loud on the unit."
- A1: "Arjun had a big meltdown before school, he slept badly and we had a substitute driver today."

## 10. Success check
- Can a judge watch a spoken sentence become a correct structured record in under 5 seconds?
- Does every pattern and answer show evidence counts that match the seeded data?
- Does a red-flag question ("he has been hitting his head") produce the doctor/care team message first?

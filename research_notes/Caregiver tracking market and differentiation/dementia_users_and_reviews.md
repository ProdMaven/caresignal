# Dementia caregivers: voices, app reviews, abandonment, unmet needs (as of Nov 14, 2026)

Scope note: about 19 tool calls. The evidence is strongest on WeCareAdvisor/DICE (2026 RCT), VoiceLove, CareZone's fate, CareMobi (2026 JMIR study), and forum voices on aide handoffs. Coverage of Jointly, ianacare, Lotsa, CaringBridge, Wellthy, Cariloop, Elsy, ForDementia, the sensor companies, Reddit specifically, and clinician log preferences is thin or missing. These are listed under Gaps, not filled from memory.

## 1. Caregiving coordination apps: reviews, fate, why families stop

### Takeaway
Existing family-coordination apps (Caring Village, Lotsa, CaringBridge) are built around calendars, tasks, med lists and journals. The praise they get is about "keeping everyone on the same page" instead of group texting. None of the sources reviewed offer structured behavior logging or pattern finding. CareZone, the best-known med and care app (about 3.5M users), had its technology bought by Walmart in 2020. That is a sign of how fragile standalone consumer caregiving apps are.

### Cited Findings
- **CareZone:** Walmart bought CareZone's technology platform, patents and IP in June 2020, and 25 to 30 product and dev staff joined Walmart. CNBC estimated the price at about $200M. CareZone kept its pharmacy business as a separate company. The app had about 3.5M users. Founded 2012, it had raised $168M (Crunchbase), with Marc Benioff among its investors. The article does not say what happened to the app or its users. — [Retail Dive, Jun 16 2020](https://www.retaildive.com/news/walmart-buys-medication-management-tech-from-startup-carezone/579916); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/06/15/walmart-buys-tech-from-carezone-to-help-people-manage-prescriptions.html)
- An archived carezone.com from Aug 2023 still showed a marketing page (scanned med lists, reminders, free monthly med delivery, "CareFlow" pharmacy platform, Medicare insurance brokers) with no shutdown notice. CareZone had pivoted toward pharmacy and insurance. — [Wayback snapshot, Aug 23 2023](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20230823150449/https://carezone.com)
- An unrelated-looking "Medisafe Carezone: PillMemo" listing now exists on the App Store, which suggests the name has been reused. Not verified. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6499107849)
- **Caring Village (iOS):** rated 4.5/5 from only 36 ratings. Free, with paid "Circle" and "Village" plans. Features include an AI assistant ("Julia"), med reminders and adherence tracking, a shared calendar, volunteer task lists, care plans, a wellness journal with symptom tracking and photos, and document storage and scanning. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caring-village/id1093814557)
  - "It's so much better than just texting each other." (eight siblings coordinating a parent's care; review dated Apr 10, year not shown) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caring-village/id1093814557)
  - "keeps everyone on the same page." (Oct 5 2025) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caring-village/id1093814557)
  - The medication section was called "a godsend." (Jan 7, year not shown) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caring-village/id1093814557)
  - The App Store page showed no negative reviews.
- **Lotsa Helping Hands:** still running as white-labeled community sites for partners such as Kaiser Permanente (kp.lotsahelpinghands.com) and a Crohn's & Colitis Foundation circle. This suggests its model has shifted toward B2B and nonprofit distribution. — [kp.lotsahelpinghands.com](https://kp.lotsahelpinghands.com/info); [CCFA partner page](https://mycancercirclenet.lotsahelpinghands.com/partners/ccfa/)
- **CareMobi** (caregiver to adult-day-center coordination; NYU 12-week feasibility study, JMIR Aging 2026):
  - 15 caregivers and 3 staff took part, and 14 of 15 logged in at least once. 75% rated it easy to use and 87.5% easy to learn.
  - Staff said it cut "phone tag."
  - Established caregivers used it passively and stayed on the phone.
  - Barriers: competing responsibilities, small staff teams, staff not wanting to enter data twice (they wanted a web or desktop version), no EHR interoperability, English-only, smartphone required.
  - Requested: clearer guidance on what to log (hydration, bathroom use), role-specific templates (daily summary, transport update), and Spanish and other languages. — [Sadarangani et al., JMIR Aging 2026;9:e85090](https://aging.jmir.org/2026/1/e85090)
  - "I'm juggling work and his appointments, some days I just can't get to it." (caregiver) — [JMIR Aging 2026](https://aging.jmir.org/2026/1/e85090)
  - "We actually got him off one of his meds, because I could prove he was eating and drinking fine there." (caregiver) — [JMIR Aging 2026](https://aging.jmir.org/2026/1/e85090)

### Inferences
- The main reason caregivers stop logging is time and effort ("some days I just can't get to it") plus entering the same data twice. A voice note in under 30 seconds goes straight at that barrier.
- Logs that can prove something to a clinician (the CareMobi med story) are what make logging feel worth it. CareSignal's doctor-ready summary is a direct value proposition.
- Incumbents compete on calendars and tasks. Behavior logging with pattern detection is an open niche among the apps reviewed.
- An English-only, smartphone-only design is a stated barrier. Multilingual voice input is a differentiator worth naming.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm when or why the CareZone consumer app shut down. The 2020 sources and the 2023 snapshot do not show a shutdown.
- I did not retrieve reviews or status for ianacare, Carely, Jointly (Carers UK), CaringBridge, Wellthy, Cariloop, Elsy or ForDementia.
- Caring Village's App Store page showed only positive reviews. Complaints would need Google Play or AppFollow review scraping, which I did not do.
- No source gave quantified abandonment rates for consumer caregiving apps.

## 2. WeCareAdvisor / DICE, VoiceLove, and other behavior-tracking tools

### Takeaway
WeCareAdvisor (DICE: Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate) is the only RCT-tested app for managing dementia behavioral symptoms. In 2026 it showed small to moderate cuts in caregiver distress and in behaviors. But caregivers used it only about once a month, which makes it a guided problem-solving tool, not a daily log. VoiceLove (Texas A&M, ARPA-H) uses video plus AI to find patterns, is in Phase I, and is not available to the public.

### Cited Findings
- **WeCareAdvisor RCT design:** a randomized trial of immediate vs delayed app use among primary family caregivers, with interviews at baseline, 1 month and 3 months. Published in J Am Geriatr Soc (DOI 10.1111/jgs.70637). Funded by NIA (R01AG061116). Run by UC Davis (Kales, senior author) and Drexel (Gitlin, first author), with McClure, Webster and Lyketsos. — [UC Davis Health, Aug 25 2026](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-center/news/headlines/app-helps-caregivers-manage-dementia-related-behavioral-symptoms-improves-well-being/2026/08)
- **WeCareAdvisor RCT results:**
  - Primary outcome: at 3 months, caregiver distress was "modestly but significantly" lower in the immediate group.
  - Secondary outcomes: fewer behavioral symptoms, less caregiver upset and better well-being.
  - The authors call the effects small to moderate and frame the study as proof of concept. — [UC Davis Health](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-center/news/headlines/app-helps-caregivers-manage-dementia-related-behavioral-symptoms-improves-well-being/2026/08)
- **WeCareAdvisor usage:** caregivers used the app about once a month on average while managing about six challenging behaviors a month (agitation, restlessness, wandering). — [UC Davis Health](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-center/news/headlines/app-helps-caregivers-manage-dementia-related-behavioral-symptoms-improves-well-being/2026/08)
- **WeCareAdvisor features:** guided questions to identify behaviors and their causes, building and evaluating non-drug strategies, daily supportive text-message tips, and education materials. — [UC Davis Health](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-center/news/headlines/app-helps-caregivers-manage-dementia-related-behavioral-symptoms-improves-well-being/2026/08)
- Gitlin: "Using the app reduced caregiver distress with these behaviors even though they still occurred." — [UC Davis Health](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-center/news/headlines/app-helps-caregivers-manage-dementia-related-behavioral-symptoms-improves-well-being/2026/08)
- **VoiceLove (Texas A&M School of Public Health; Junhyoung Kim, Marcia Ory, Dan Zhang):**
  - What it does: caregivers and therapists record short videos of daily interactions. AI finds individual patterns in speech, nonverbal behavior, needs, triggers and responses to activities, and turns them into insights clinicians can use for personalized plans. Covers autistic children and older adults with dementia.
  - Funding: one of three organizations picked for ARPA-H funding. The amount was not stated.
  - Status: Phase I. After IRB approval, a small pilot of about one month will test usability, followed by a year of refinement. Availability was not stated. — [Texas A&M Today, Jul 22 2026](https://stories.tamu.edu/news/2026/07/22/ai-powered-tool-aims-to-aid-communication-for-people-with-autism-and-dementia/)
  - A related ARPA-H award page is titled "ACE-AI: Adaptive Communication Enhancement for Cognitive Disabilities through Advanced AI Integration." I did not confirm it is the same project. — [ARPA-H](https://arpa-h.gov/explore-funding/awards/4001)

### Inferences
- WeCareAdvisor shows the DICE logic works. But at about once a month, it captures reflective problem-solving, not the in-the-moment data across days and caregivers that pattern statistics need. CareSignal can position itself as the daily capture layer that feeds DICE-style "Investigate" with real data. CareSignal's ranked "possible things to check" lines up with DICE's non-drug approach.
- VoiceLove is the closest conceptual competitor (autism plus dementia, AI pattern finding). It is video-based, clinician-facing and years from release. Voice-first and open to the whole family is a clear contrast.

### Gaps
- The WeCareAdvisor press release gives no sample size, effect sizes, public availability or licensing. I did not confirm whether DICE or WeCareAdvisor is commercially licensed. Checking the JAGS paper (DOI above) and the DICE website would settle it.
- Sensor and AI eldercare companies (CarePredict, Vayyar, Cera, Sensi.ai, Inspiren): not researched in this pass, so I have no cited details on what they track.

## 3. How families track and share today (forums)

### Takeaway
Families improvise: paper binders and communication books in the home, whiteboards, private Facebook groups and WhatsApp. Handoffs between aides and between family members are the pain point. Some caregivers are told to "keep records of everything" (often for caregiver agreements) and cannot find an easy way. Writing notes in front of the person with dementia can itself cause conflict.

### Cited Findings
- "Looking for an easy way for our two care givers and myself to share daily information regarding the experiences with my wife." (howhale, Nov 2024) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "I made a daily log which was kept in a binder in the home." (fesk, Nov 2024) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "When one aide left and another came in, there was a clear picture of what happened during the day." (fesk, Nov 2024) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "It was near impossible to record notes in a book or to read it without it being an issue with my wife." howhale (Nov 2024) moved to a private Facebook group, which also keeps the hospice nurse informed. — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "they likely have a communication book already." (harshedbuzz, on agency aides, Nov 2024) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "WhatsApp would work assuming the aides are open to its use." (harshedbuzz, Nov 2024) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- One commenter raised HIPAA as a possible concern for paid caregivers using electronic messaging (fmb, Nov 2024; the commenter's own understanding). — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/comment/228341)
- "I was told to keep records of everything I am doing and the times they were done." and "I am looking for an easy way to keep track of this." (mandy_r, Dec 2024, thread on a timestamped log for a caregiver agreement) — [ALZConnected](https://alzconnected.org/discussion/71392/caregiver-daily-log-with-timestamps-for-a-caregiver-agreement)
- **Whiteboard and sibling friction (AgingCare / caregivers.com, date not shown):**
  - The board "lists today's date, Mom's weight, AM and PM pills taken, food eaten, if a shower was given" plus who Mom talked to, appointments and bills.
  - "When sister calls now she asks Mom to look at the board and tell her about her day."
  - The thread started from arguments with a sister whose questions upset Mom. — [caregivers.com thread](https://portal.prod.caregivers.com/discussions/tip-for-appeasing-sibling-168159.htm)
- **Sundowning and burnout (Mayo Clinic Connect, Jun to Jul 2024):**
  - "Sundowning is real; you can practically set your watch by it." (billiekip)
  - "The wild goose chases have ceased to be amusing." (billiekip, on hiding and losing items)
  - "I am not totally burned out, because I still function and get everything accomplished" (chris20) — [Mayo Clinic Connect](https://connect.mayoclinic.org/comment/1110039)

### Inferences
- "You can set your watch by it" sundowning is exactly the time-of-day pattern that simple statistics on timestamped logs can surface and confirm.
- Binders fail when the reader is not in the room (remote siblings), and written notes can upset the person with dementia. Discreet voice capture and a shared digital timeline solve both. Privacy and HIPAA worries with aides suggest giving aides an invite-only role.
- Siblings who see only part of the day (or only phone calls) get into conflict. A shared, objective timeline is the de-escalation tool.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Reddit threads directly (r/dementia, r/AgingParents, r/CaregiverSupport). No verbatim Reddit quotes are included.
- I found no sources in this pass on multilingual or immigrant families caring for parents, or on Carers UK forum voices.
- I did not capture quotes about aides failing to pass on information. The forum evidence covers the workarounds, not the failures.

## 4. What clinicians want from caregiver logs

### Takeaway
Only indirect evidence: caregivers find logs useful for preparing for provider visits and for persuading clinicians (CareMobi), and caregivers are told to keep timed records. I found no direct survey of geriatricians or neurologists on log formats.

### Cited Findings
- Routine ADC updates helped caregivers prepare for provider visits. One caregiver used logged eating and drinking data to get a medication stopped. — [JMIR Aging 2026](https://aging.jmir.org/2026/1/e85090)
- VoiceLove is built around insights that "clinicians can use to build personalized intervention plans," which implies clinicians want pattern summaries more than raw notes. — [Texas A&M Today, Jul 2026](https://stories.tamu.edu/news/2026/07/22/ai-powered-tool-aims-to-aid-communication-for-people-with-autism-and-dementia/)

### Inferences
- A one-page summary (frequency, time of day, triggers tried, what helped, red flags) is likely what makes logs worth a clinician's time. This needs validation.

### Gaps
- No primary evidence (surveys, interviews) on geriatrician or neurologist preferences for caregiver behavior logs, such as NPI-style frequency and severity formats. Suggested follow-up: PubMed search for "caregiver diary" and "neuropsychiatric symptoms" with "clinician".
